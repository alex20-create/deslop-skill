# English: lists, examples and norm rules

Read with `SKILL.md`. Codes match. **Keep** examples show text that stays as it is; **Before/After** examples show the minimal fix. Never convert British spelling to American or the reverse unless `SKILL.md` allows it.

## Group R: residue

Only when addressed to the person who wrote the prompt or when the model talks about itself (see `SKILL.md`). In an email the draft writes to someone else, "Here is the schedule" or "I hope this helps" is content. In dialogue, a character who says "I'm sorry, but I can't" is not the model.

**R1. Watch for:** Of course!, Certainly!, Absolutely!, Great question!, You're absolutely right, Here is, Here's a, I hope this helps, Would you like me to..., Want me to...?, Let me know if, Feel free to ask, Good luck!

> **Before:** Certainly! The shoot starts on Monday at 8 am at the studio. I hope this helps!
> **After:** The shoot starts on Monday at 8 am at the studio.

**R2. Watch for:** as an AI language model, as an AI, I cannot, I'm sorry, but, as of my last training update, up to my knowledge cutoff, based on the information available to me.

**R3. Watch for:** here is a draft, this version keeps the tone you asked for, I have structured it into, this replaces, revised version:.

**R4. Watch for:** [Your Name], [Company Name], [insert date], [figure], XX%, Company X, Person Y.

**R5. Watch for:** utm_source=chatgpt.com, utm_source=openai, oaicite, contentReference, turn0search0, "Subject:" inside the body, Markdown (`**`, `##`, `- `) in a plain-text email, form or script.

## Group I: invented and unsupported content

**I2. Watch for:** likely grew up, probably began, it is believed that, suggesting that, appears to have (filling a gap); information is not publicly available, not widely documented, maintains a low profile.

> **Before:** Information about her early life is not publicly available, suggesting she maintains a low profile. She likely grew up around the film industry.
> **After:** (Remove both sentences and list the gap under "To check".)

**I3. Watch for (unnamed authority, always acts):** experts agree, studies show, research suggests, critics have praised, observers note, industry reports. **Vague links (list only when they hide a real relationship):** linked to, associated with, connected to.

> **Before:** Experts agree that the festival draws more than 100,000 visitors a year.
> **After:** The festival draws more than 100,000 visitors a year. (List the figure under "To check".)

**I5. Watch for:** award-winning, leading, renowned, world-class, pioneering, the first, the largest; intensifiers to remove: undisputed, truly, genuinely.

> **Before:** The school is a truly world-class centre for animation.
> **After:** The school is a world-class centre for animation. (List "world-class".)

## Group E: style habits

### E1. Not X but Y

**Watch for:** not X but Y; not just, not only or not merely X, but Y; it's not X, it's Y; "This does not mean X. It means Y."; a clipped tail (", no guessing").

> **Keep:** It isn't a remake; it's a prequel. (Corrects what the reader would assume.)
> **Before:** The app sorts tasks by project. It's not just a tool, it's a new way of working.
> **After:** The app sorts tasks by project.

### E2. Closers and fragments

**Watch for:** "That is the real win."; "And that changes everything."; "Simple as that."; "Let that sink in."; "Nothing was ever the same."; the same closer after several sections; fragments with no information ("No fear. No excuses. No limits.").

> **Keep:** Then the new algorithm arrived. No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia. No inherited rules. (Each fragment adds something.)
> **Before:** Then the new algorithm arrived. No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia. No inherited rules. Nothing was ever the same.
> **After:** Then the new algorithm arrived. No aesthetic prior. No nostalgia. No inherited rules.

### E3. Sayings that sound deep

**Watch for:** the real question is, at its core, what really matters, the heart of the matter, X is the language of Y, X becomes a trap.

> **Keep:** The edit is the final rewrite of the script. (A known idea in the trade, stated deliberately.)
> **Before:** Symmetry is the language of trust. Users prefer symmetric layouts because they feel predictable.
> **After:** Users prefer symmetric layouts because they feel predictable.

### E4. Run-ups

**Watch for:** Let's dive in, Let's break this down, Here's what you need to know, Without further ado, Here's the thing, Let's be honest, Real talk, Honestly?

> **Keep (social post):** Here's the thing about shooting outdoors in November: the light is gone by four.
> **Before (informative):** Here's what you need to know. Applications close on 15 March.
> **After:** Applications close on 15 March.

### E5. Arguing with no one

**Watch for:** This isn't about, I'm not saying, To be clear, Don't get me wrong, This is not to say, Some might say... but, A tempting approach would be.

> **Before:** I'm not saying documentation doesn't matter. What matters is whether the crew can use the note on set.
> **After:** What matters is whether the crew can use the note on set.

### E6. Triads by default

**Watch for:** three abstract nouns in a row (innovation, inspiration and insight), three-item lists in sentence after sentence.

> **Keep:** The festival includes screenings, talks and industry meetings. (Three real items.)
> **Before:** The festival includes screenings, talks and industry meetings. It offers inspiration, learning and connection.
> **After:** The festival includes screenings, talks and industry meetings.

### E7. Repeated openings

> **Keep:** We shot at dawn. We shot at dusk. We shot until the money ran out. (Deliberate anaphora.)
> **Before:** The team reviewed the plan. The team adjusted the schedule. The team sent the call sheet.
> **After:** The team reviewed the plan, adjusted the schedule and sent the call sheet.

### E8. Dashes as the universal connector

Act when there are several em dashes, spaced en dashes or double hyphens doing the work of commas, colons or brackets. Replace them with a comma, colon, full stop or brackets. En dashes in ranges (1990–1995) are correct and stay. If the writer's own sample uses dashes, keep them at the same rate.

> **Before:** The new policy — announced without warning — affects thousands of workers — and arrives late.
> **After:** The new policy, announced without warning, affects thousands of workers and arrives late.

### E9. Stacked qualifiers

**Watch for:** to be fair, it's also possible, could potentially, might arguably, in some cases it may, piled on one claim.

> **Before:** It could potentially be argued that the policy might have some effect on outcomes.
> **After:** The policy might have some effect on outcomes.

### E10. AI vocabulary in clusters

**Watch for:** additionally, align with, bolster, crucial, deep dive, delve, empower, enduring, enhance, foster, garner, highlight (verb), interplay, intricate, key (adjective), landscape (abstract), meticulous, pivotal, robust (figurative), seamless, showcase, tapestry (abstract), testament, underscore (verb), vibrant.

> **Keep:** The deadline is crucial for the grant. (One word, ordinary use.)
> **Before:** Additionally, the school plays a pivotal role in vibrant regional film, fostering technical training.
> **After:** The school fosters technical training in regional film.
<!-- new-words: fosters -->

### E11. Inflated significance

**Watch for:** stands as a testament, a pivotal moment, plays a key role, marked a turning point, enduring legacy, set the stage for, evolving landscape, indelible mark, the future looks bright, a step in the right direction; "Despite these challenges, ... continues to thrive".

> **Before:** The institute was founded in 1989, marking a pivotal moment for regional statistics.
> **After:** The institute was founded in 1989.

### E12. Shallow -ing riders

**Watch for:** highlighting, underscoring, emphasising, ensuring, reflecting, symbolising, contributing to, cultivating, fostering, showcasing, at the end of a sentence about something else.

> **Before:** The cinema reopened in 2021, reflecting the community's enduring love of film.
> **After:** The cinema reopened in 2021.

### E13. Sales language with nothing behind it

**Watch for:** nestled, in the heart of, breathtaking, stunning, must-visit, hidden gem, rich (figurative), unforgettable experience, diverse array.

> **Keep (promotional):** Three nights of horror in a 1932 picture house. (Persuasive and concrete.)
> **Before:** Nestled in the heart of the hills, this breathtaking village has 400 residents.
> **After:** This village in the hills has 400 residents.

### E14. Grand verbs

**Watch for:** serves as, stands as, functions as, represents (for "is"); boasts, features, offers, houses (for "has"). "Offers" and "features" are ordinary in promotional English: act only when grand verbs pile up.

> **Before:** The venue serves as the area's main cinema and boasts three screens.
> **After:** The venue is the area's main cinema and has three screens.

### E19. Elegant variation

**Watch for:** the director, the filmmaker, the auteur, the creative mind, all for one person in consecutive sentences.

### E20. False ranges

**Watch for:** from X to Y, ranging from X to Y, everything from X to Y, when X and Y are not ends of a scale.

> **Before:** The programme ranges from international premieres to restored classics.
> **After:** The programme includes international premieres and restored classics.

### E21. Didactic asides and summaries

**Watch for:** it is important to note, it is worth noting, it should be noted, keep in mind that; In summary, In conclusion, Overall as an opener that restates.

> **Before:** It is important to note that entry is free on Wednesdays.
> **After:** Entry is free on Wednesdays.

## Group N: norm corrections

### N1. Capitalisation

Headings: follow the writer's or publication's style (sentence case or title case) and keep it consistent across the text. Do not capitalise common nouns for emphasis.

### N2. Punctuation and numbers

- Quotation marks: keep the writer's system. British publishing often uses single quotes first ('...', nesting "..." inside), American double ("...", nesting '...' inside). Correct nesting is one system. Mixing systems at the same level in one text is the error.
- Keep one style for numbers, dates and times throughout (15 March or March 15; 8 am or 8:00).

### N3. Dashes

Ranges take an en dash (1990–1995) or a hyphen consistently. Do not mix em dashes and spaced en dashes as connectors in one text: list it and follow the writer's majority. Spaced en dashes as connectors are common in British typography; keep them if the writer uses them consistently and sparingly (see E8).

### N5. Hyphenated compounds

Keep the hyphen before a noun (a high-quality report) and drop it after (the report is high quality). Compounds with an -ly adverb take no hyphen (a highly rated report). Words always hyphenated in the dictionary (third-party) keep it.

### N6. Spelling and agreement

Keep the variety's spelling consistent (colour/color, organise/organize, programme/program); if the draft mixes them without reason, list it and do not unify on your own. Correct typos and agreement errors and list them.
